Two years ago, fewer than a quarter of small businesses had touched a generative AI tool. Now more than half have, and roughly one in two small-business workers uses AI at their job on any given week. That's a faster climb than the Chamber of Commerce has tracked for any workplace technology in decades.

And yet, ask those same owners if they'd let AI handle a routine task — chasing an invoice, triaging a lead, drafting a customer reply — without a human checking the output first, and most say no. 78% of small business owners don't fully trust AI to run low-level tasks unsupervised, even the ones they admit would save them real time.

That's not a contradiction. It's the most honest data point in small-business AI right now.

## The adoption curve is real

The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's Technology Engagement Center, working with Teneo Research, has been tracking generative AI use among small businesses since 2023. The number has more than doubled twice over: 23% in 2023, 40% in 2024, 58% in 2025.

[chart:0]

The Chamber's separate Main Street AI Monitor — a quarterly Ipsos survey of over a thousand small-business employees — found roughly half of all workers at small businesses were already using AI on the job as of May 2026, mostly to save time and sharpen their output, not to automate anyone out of a role.

Bluevine's 2026 survey of U.S. owners, fielded in April, puts engagement even higher: 74% say they're actively using or testing AI. Among Bluevine's own customers, usage of Anthropic's Claude grew 729% between 2025 and 2026, overtaking ChatGPT in monthly transactions by April — a sign the tools businesses reach for are shifting, not just multiplying.

## But confidence hasn't caught up

That same Bluevine research found 82% of owners hit a real roadblock trying to go deeper with AI. Asked what's actually stopping them, the top two answers were data security and privacy (33%, up 10 points from a year earlier) and distrust of AI's accuracy (31%) — both ahead of cost.

[chart:1]

Anthropic surveyed small business owners ahead of launching Claude for Small Business in May 2026 and landed on nearly the same answer from a different angle: half named data security as their single biggest hesitation about adopting AI at all.

Put the findings side by side and a pattern falls out. Small business owners aren't AI-skeptical — they're delegation-skeptical, the same instinct that makes a founder personally sign every check early on, now applied to software that can draft the check, read the inbox, and update the CRM on its own.

## Why the caution is rational, not backward

A chatbot draft that needs editing is a low-stakes mistake. An agent that reads customer records or moves money unsupervised is a different risk category — and small businesses have far less legal and IT infrastructure than an enterprise to absorb a bad outcome.

That's the gap the newest small-business AI products are explicitly built to close. Claude for Small Business ships 15 pre-built workflows across finance, sales, and customer service, but Anthropic's own framing leads with control: every workflow needs owner approval before it runs, or runs end-to-end only once the owner chooses, and existing access permissions carry over so the AI can't see data an employee couldn't. That's a direct answer to the 33% and the 50%, not a workaround for them.

## Closing the gap without closing your eyes

The businesses getting real value aren't the ones that trusted AI first — they're the ones that gave it a job small enough to check.

- Start with one task that has a visible, checkable output — a drafted reply, a flagged invoice — not one that touches money or customer data end-to-end.
- Keep a human approving the output for the first month. You're building trust data, not just saving time.
- Match access to the task: a connector that reads your CRM doesn't need permission to send from it too.
- Expand only once you can point to a number — hours saved, invoices caught — not a feeling that it's helping.

## The takeaway

Small businesses didn't move slowly on AI — they moved faster than on almost any technology before it. What they've been careful about is handing over the keys, and the data says that instinct is sound: the same surveys that show adoption surging also show data security sitting at the top of every list of reasons to hold back. Closing that gap isn't about businesses trusting AI more blindly; it's about building the kind of scoped, reviewable workflows that make trust unnecessary to ask for — which is the same groundwork brandbizkit's AI transformation guidance starts with.
