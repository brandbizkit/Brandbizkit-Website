Picture a customer typing a question into ChatGPT: "Who's a good [your service] near me?" Three names come back. If yours isn't one of them, you never even knew you were in the race.

That's no longer a niche behavior. It's becoming the default.

## The shift happened in twelve months

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, covering 1,002 U.S. adults, found that use of generative AI tools for local business recommendations rose from 6% to 45% in a year. That made AI the third most popular source of recommendations.

[chart:1]

Google didn't disappear, though. The same survey shows Google's share of review sources slipping from 83% to 71%, while it stays well ahead of every other platform.

## Customers use AI as a first step, not the last

Here's the nuance. In BrightLocal's Small Business Marketing Report 2026, 23% of consumers used AI during their most recent local search. Only 2% stopped there. 43% went on to Google, and 39% looked at social media.

So AI is often the shortlist, and everything else is the background check. Your business has to show up in the shortlist *and* survive the check.

## Owners know — and most say they're acting

The same report, published October 21, 2026, surveyed 1,002 owners of local businesses with 1–50 employees. 86% know consumers use AI tools to find and choose local businesses, and 65% say they're actively working on AI search visibility.

But here's the catch. Only 54% have checked and are confident they appear in AI results. Another 21% think they probably do, but haven't looked. Put together, 46% haven't checked or don't know.

## The boring basics still feed the answer

Most owners already have a Google Business Profile — 85% do. The trouble is upkeep. Only 35% keep it active. 41% keep their website active, and just 26% stay active on other review sites and directories.

[chart:2]

BrightLocal's own framing is that owners are chasing AI visibility while neglecting the channels that feed it. That's our reading of the numbers too, with one caveat: BrightLocal sells local-marketing software, so treat its framing with healthy skepticism. The raw figures still point the same way.

## What to do this week

You don't need a new tool. You need a short checklist:

- **Ask the AI yourself.** Search your service and city in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Note who appears and why.
- **Refresh your Google Business Profile.** Current hours, services, photos, and a reply to your latest reviews.
- **Make your website say what you do.** Plain-language service pages, location, pricing ranges and FAQs that answer the questions customers actually type.
- **Keep reviews flowing** on more than one platform.

## The takeaway

AI search doesn't replace the fundamentals. It rewards them. The businesses that get recommended are the ones whose information is current, consistent, and easy for a machine to understand.
